# Cold Outreach Framework — how a single message is built

> The message-construction layer of Meagan's outreach stack. GIVE-FIRST-METHOD.md
> says WHO to reach and WHEN (circles, beats, the three-part opener). BRAND-VOICE.md
> says how she SOUNDS. This file says how one message is physically constructed:
> subject lines, word counts, line order, channel differences, follow-up cadence.
> Apply it to every cold email, LinkedIn DM, connect note, and follow-up you draft.
>
> This is the packaged default (repo-owned, editable prose — update it as the
> inbox changes). If Meagan files her own copy in the vault as
> `Just Fractional/COLD-OUTREACH-FRAMEWORK.md`, that copy wins.
> Grounded in mid-2026 outreach data; revisit ~quarterly.

---

## The state of the inbox (why this framework is strict)

- A decision-maker now gets ~120 emails a day and roughly half are AI-written.
  Anything that smells templated is deleted in ~3 seconds. Generic cold runs
  1–3% reply; signal-led, genuinely specific outreach runs 5–18%. *Specificity
  beats volume* is no longer a taste preference — it's the measured spread.
- Buyers discount obvious AI personalization ("Congrats on the raise!" +
  first-line flattery is now read as machine output). Manually-edited messages
  out-reply fully-automated ones. Every draft must read like Meagan typed it
  on her phone between meetings.
- Follow-ups produce 50–70% of total replies. A single send is half a campaign —
  always draft the first follow-up at the same time as the opener.

## The universal skeleton (any channel)

Four moves, in order. This is the three-part opener from GIVE-FIRST-METHOD.md
rendered at the sentence level, plus an optional proof beat:

1. **Signal line** (the warmth connection) — ≤15 words, present tense, the real
   reason you're reaching out *today*. Answers "why you, why now" before they
   can ask it. Never a creepy signal (profile view, email open).
2. **Proof-of-work line** (the personal reference) — one specific thing about
   *them* that couldn't be Googled in five seconds and couldn't be said to
   anyone else. This line earns the read.
3. **The bridge** — at most ONE sentence connecting what you saw to a pain they
   feel *this week* (hair on fire, not smoke; specific, not categorical).
   When proof helps, fold in ONE number (+92% activation, 6 hrs → 12 min) —
   never a list of them.
4. **The either/or question** — one question, two concrete answers, present
   tense. The ask is a *reply*, not a meeting. Per Give-First, ~90% of touches
   carry no CTA at all — and the first touch never carries a calendar link,
   deck, or any link.

One idea per message. One question per message. If you can't name the circle
and the signal before drafting, the message isn't ready (and if there's no
signal at all, that's Circle 4 — don't send it).

**The swap test (run on every draft):** if you could swap in a different name
and company and the message still mostly works, it's template energy — rewrite
the proof-of-work line until the swap breaks it.

## Email (the default channel for VCs; strong for founders too)

**Subject line**
- 2–4 words, ≤33 characters, front-loaded — it gets cut on mobile after that.
- Lowercase, internal-memo energy ("activation after the raise", "ora's
  onboarding"), like a colleague wrote it. No title case, no clickbait, no
  "quick question", no merge-field stink.
- A specific noun from *their* world beats a clever phrase from yours.

**Body**
- 50–100 words. Hard cap 120. Sub-100-word emails out-reply 200-word emails
  by ~2.4x; nobody ever replied "too short."
- 1–3 sentence paragraphs, plain text only: no links, no attachments, no
  images, no signature block beyond her name (a heavy signature is a spam
  tell and a who-is-this tell).
- Mobile test: the signal line and the either/or question must both be visible
  on a phone without scrolling.

**VC flavor (Motion 1)** — email beats DM; most VCs dislike DMs. 2–3 sentences
referencing ONE specific portfolio company's likely activation/churn situation,
then the soft one-line ask ("happy to be a fractional resource you can refer
in"). No deck, no bio paragraph — the proof number carries credibility.

## LinkedIn DM (1st-degree connections and accepted requests)

- 40–80 words, 2–4 sentences. DMs are chat, not letters — a DM that looks like
  an email gets treated like one (ignored).
- Skip greeting filler ("Hope you're doing well!"). Open on the signal.
- The first line is the notification preview — make it carry the signal, not
  "Hi [Name], I wanted to reach out because…".
- First DM = Connect beat only. The pitch never appears in message one; Build
  (the curious either/or) is as far as it goes. The win condition is a reply.
- When the relationship warrants more warmth (Circle 1, or a hot Circle 2
  reply), suggest Meagan record a 30-second voice note and draft her the
  3-beat talking points instead of prose — in an inbox full of AI text, an
  unmistakably human artifact is the strongest differentiator there is.

## Connect note

- The governor's default batch workflow is **no note** (verified ICP, she
  sends). Draft a note only when there's real shared context worth naming.
- When you do: ≤200 characters, name the genuine context, zero pitch, no
  "I'd love to add you to my network." The note opens a door; it sells nothing.

## Follow-ups (where most of the replies actually live)

- **Cadence:** 2–3 follow-ups max per channel, 3–5 days apart (give VCs a full
  week). Then stop and move them to nurture — silence is an answer too.
- **Length:** ~40 words. Shorter than the opener, always.
- **Every follow-up adds something new** — a sharper observation, a relevant
  resource, one proof point not yet used. NEVER "just bumping this" /
  "floating to the top of your inbox" — a bump says *I had nothing to add*.
- A reply to your own thread (email) keeps context; a fresh subject reads as
  a fresh campaign. Reply to the thread.
- **Switch channels rather than hammering one:** two unanswered emails → a
  thoughtful comment on their post or a short DM beats email #3. The
  multi-channel pattern out-converts single-channel by a wide margin.
- **The last touch ends generously** (Give-First: bow out, recommend someone
  else if you can, leave the door open). No breakup-email guilt theater
  ("Should I close your file?").

## AI-slop kill list (delete on sight in any draft)

The 2026 inbox has trained everyone to pattern-match these as machine output:

- "I hope this finds you well" / "Quick question" / "I'll keep this brief"
- "I noticed you…" + a fact scraped from their headline
- Compliment → pivot → pitch ("Congrats on the raise! Speaking of growth…")
- Flattery about their "journey," "impressive growth," or "what you're building"
- More than one question, more than one CTA, or any bullet list in a first touch
- "synergy," "leverage," "streamline," "circle back," "touch base"
- Title-case subject lines; exclamation points doing enthusiasm's job
- Em dashes in LinkedIn content (per BRAND-VOICE — commas and periods)
- "advisor" / "consultant" — she is an **executive for rent**, always
- Any sentence that survives the swap test

## Pre-send checklist (run before save_outreach_draft)

1. Which circle, which beat? (Named, not guessed.)
2. Signal line first, present tense, non-creepy?
3. Proof-of-work line specific enough to break the swap test?
4. One either/or question — hair on fire, not smoke?
5. Word counts: email ≤120 (aim 50–100) / DM ≤80 / follow-up ~40 / subject ≤4 words?
6. Zero links in a first touch? One idea? No pitch before Build?
7. Read it aloud — does it pass BRAND-VOICE's "is this me?" test?
8. Follow-up #1 drafted alongside the opener, with something new in it?
